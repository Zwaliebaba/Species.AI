# R3a — Adoptability of the reference mechanics

Reviewer R3a, Phase 5. Scope: every document under `design_next/reference/`, read cold, with `design_next/glossary/`, `design_next/CONVENTIONS.md` and the decision register (`design_next/decisions/`) as the only other sources. `design/` was not read.

Method. Each reference document was opened at its template headings, its §3 diagrams, its §4 tuning table and its §8; at least two requirements per document were then sampled (more in the large files), favouring states, timers, formulas and cross-document dependencies, and each was tested against one question: can a designer adopt, adapt or drop it by id without reading anything else? The test covered terms (defined in the document or in the glossary), numbers (in a tuning table with a key and a unit), states (entry and exit), dependencies (named by id) and Open DECs (stating the behaviour to build). Three helper scripts (`species_work/scripts/R3a_extract.py`, `R3a_checks.py`, `R3a_show.py`, run with `python3 -I`) extracted the requirements, tuning rows, DEC entries and diagrams, and ran structural scans over the whole folder: cited keys that are undefined (none found), tuning rows without unit or value, numbers in requirement text, Open DECs cited by rules but missing from §8, state diagrams with sinks or orphan states, and glossary coverage of terms used in the requirements. Every scan hit reported below was then checked by hand.

Overall verdict. The reference documents are adoptable. Of the 225 requirements sampled, 174 can be adopted by id as they stand: their numbers are keyed, their states are complete and their dependencies are named by id. The other 51 carry a gap, most of them Low (a restatement without its owner id, a tick-rate literal, a composite key). The gaps found are concentrated in four places: the glossary does not cover the session vocabulary of the replicated simulation; restated rules have no rule for which id wins; a handful of formulas embed the tick rate as a bare literal; and the music rules of reference/10 disagree with their own Open DEC. One High finding: a value in the overview contradicts its owner.

## Structural scans (whole folder)

- **Cited tuning keys.** Every `ref.` key that a reference document cites in backticks exists in a tuning table. The only unmatched `ref.` tokens are the key-family names in the prose of screen_flow_and_match_setup.md §4.1 (`ref.meta.front_end`, `ref.meta.layout` and the others), which are families, not keys.
- **Tuning rows without a unit.** 121 reference rows have "—" as their unit. Almost all are dimensionless factors, divisors, odds or blend weights, and their notes say how they are used. Row 54 is the one that needs a unit; rows 52 and 53 have one, but in seconds where CONVENTIONS §5 asks for ticks.
- **Keys read by no rule.** 22 rows are marked "unused", "read by no rule" or "not used in a match" (for example `ref.unit.spider.normal_speed`, `ref.unit.*.rate` of kinds that never shoot, `ref.weapon.turret_round.life_by_level`). Each is stated explicitly, so the design phase can drop it. No finding.
- **Approximate values in MUSTs.** 53 reference requirements contain "about N", almost all of them derived consequences of keyed rules (for example "about 4 health per second"). Only R-MODE-412 (row 39) is a MUST made solely of approximations.
- **State diagrams.** 87 `stateDiagram-v2` blocks in the reference documents. The scan found five with a sink or an orphan state. Rows 41 to 44 are real; the fifth, the team-kind figure of reference/12 06, is correct, because Bot and Spectator are kinds that last until the match ends.
- **Open DECs.** 134 Open DECs are cited by reference requirements. Seventeen were flagged by the wording of their specified behaviour ("not established", "not recorded", "assumed"). On reading, DEC-312, DEC-317 and DEC-327 lack a usable behaviour to build (rows 58 to 60); DEC-205, which the scan did not flag, has none for host loss (row 16, found by reading R-META-092); and DEC-049 contradicts DEC-097 (row 12). The other fourteen (DEC-108, DEC-240, DEC-248, DEC-300, DEC-301, DEC-305, DEC-307, DEC-308, DEC-311, DEC-315, DEC-321, DEC-324, DEC-330, DEC-802) do state the behaviour that the documents specify; only their intent or how often the case occurs is unknown.
