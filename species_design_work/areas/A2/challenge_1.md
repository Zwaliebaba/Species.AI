## The weakest point

The gate-B budgets look about three times roomier than they are. A2 reports a world of 200 stewards at its stress case as 35 % of an 8-vCPU host and treats region hosts as a reserve "for measured costs above about twice the budgets" (§4.4), but A2's own tick wall budget (ADR-080 item 6: a tick finishes within 50 % of its 100 ms period) allows only 240 reference-milliseconds per tick on that host, so the same load is already 70 % of it; with region steps scheduled as the single-threaded jobs that ADR-083 item 6 makes them, the tick takes 38.6 ms on 8 workers and 43.8 ms when one vCPU serves the tick thread, network and store, and the host misses its budget at 1.30 and 1.14 times the per-figure costs, which is inside ADR-080's own confirmation tolerance of 25 %. Those costs are unmeasured targets taken from a guidance figure that DBG-205 lets the design phase read as a whole tick (A2's reading) or as a per-frame share of a tick that spans six frames, which is six times more. Both likely corrections then break the plan that gate B is asked to confirm: a cost overrun, whose stated remedy shrinks battles (pillar 1, PIL-021) although watched settlements are 78 % of the cost; and a move to 20 ticks per second, which ADR-079's own latency confirmation is likely to trigger (direct control p95 331 ms on the integrated host against its 250 ms threshold, once the client-side terms the model omits are counted), doubling the server CPU and lifting the integrated host's upload above its 4 Mbit/s default. Gate B would confirm caps and host specifications on numbers whose real margin is about 1.2 times.

## Findings

### A2-F01 — The stage-3 margin is 1.14 to 1.30 times, not about twice

- **Severity:** High · **Category:** scale and cost
- **Where:** ADR-080 items 6 and 8; §4.4 "What 200 does not need"; §4.5 Table 8 and "What the results mean"; ADR-083 item 6.
- **Failure scenario.** The owner confirms at gate B a hosted world of 200 on 8 vCPU, 2 GB and 50 Mbit/s, on the strength of "35 % of an 8-vCPU host" and a distribution reserve "for measured costs above about twice the budgets". The simulation-core milestone then measures the per-figure costs at 1.2 times the budgets, which ADR-080's confirmation accepts as "within 25 %". The first 200-steward world at its evening peak needs 43.8 ms per tick on a host with one vCPU for the tick thread, network I/O and the store thread (ADR-083 item 6 puts network I/O and the store on threads of their own), and 52.6 ms at ×1.2: every evening tick overruns ADR-080 item 6, owed ticks accumulate, and ADR-083 item 7 re-anchors the schedule every 20 owed ticks, so world time runs slower than real time for every steward during the hours that matter. The record says the mitigation (region hosts) is a later record of stage 3, so the world has none.
- **Numbers.** The model's 167.3 reference-ms per tick is 35 % of 480 reference-ms (8 × 0.6 × 100 ms), but the wall budget is 50 ms, so the tick may use 240 reference-ms: 70 % before load balance and serial parts. Region steps are single-threaded jobs (ADR-083 item 6), the busiest is 4.81 reference-ms (8.0 ms on a vCPU), and the world owner's step is serial after them. Longest-processing-time scheduling of the 77 region jobs gives 38.6 ms (8 workers) and 43.8 ms (7 workers); the budget breaks at ×1.30 and ×1.14. A 16-vCPU host holds to ×2.52. The community host of stage 2 (2 vCPU) breaks at ×2.01 with 16 stewards all present. The vCPU factor of 0.6 is unmeasured, and DEC-971 pins the reference test machine as the first machine that meets the minima, which may be a modern developer PC or a 2013-class AVX2 CPU; at 0.4 reference cores per vCPU the 200 stress case is 52 % of the host before any wall budget. Separately, a region at its body budget takes 8.43 of its 10 ms, a headroom of ×1.186, so a cost that ADR-080's confirmation accepts (+25 %) puts that region at 10.54 ms, above its own step budget.
- **Recommendation.** Add the wall time to the model and to its `check` (region jobs on the host's workers, the serial world step, the tick thread's own vCPU) and state every host specification by it, with the reserve threshold that results (×1.3 on 8 vCPU, not "about twice"). Offer gate B either 16 vCPU for 200 stewards (×2.52) or the 8-vCPU host with a stated region-host fallback designed before stage 3 starts. Make ADR-080's confirmation consistent with its budgets: either a tolerance of 15 %, or a region body budget that holds at +25 % (3,795 bodies at the battle cost, rounded to 3,750). State the vCPU factor as a range (0.4 to 1.0) until DEC-971 pins the machine, and show Table 8 at both ends.

### A2-F02 — The remedy for a cost overrun cuts battles, and the cost basis may be six times low

- **Severity:** High · **Category:** scale and cost
- **Where:** ADR-080 item 2 (the 2.1 µs basis), Confirmation ("lowers the region body budget and the battle size by the ratio"); §4.5 "What the results mean"; DBG-205; DEC-228 item 1; PIL-021.
- **Failure scenario.** The simulation-core milestone measures a battle figure at 8 µs per tick instead of 2.1, plausible because the reference spreads each tick over ten slices and six frames (R-SIM-510 to R-SIM-534) precisely because a tick is too expensive for one frame, and because DBG-205 leaves open whether 4.2 ms is the cost of a tick or of the frame that carries one sixth of it (DEC-228 item 1; A2 chose the whole-tick reading without saying that the other reading is 25.2 ms per tick). ADR-080's confirmation then lowers the battle size and the region body budget "by the ratio": the battle cap falls from 1,000 to about 260 and the region body budget from 4,000 to about 1,050, below PIL-021's typical 400 and just above its floor of 200, and a region can no longer hold four settlements of 600 (WLD-281), so A1 must drop sites per region. Pillar 1 is lost to fix a cost that battles do not cause.
- **Numbers.** At the budgets, watched settlements at full fidelity are 131.0 of 167.3 ms at 200 (78 %) and 19.2 of 28.3 ms at 16; battles are 23.6 and 7.3 ms. At ×4, keeping every battle at 400 figures costs 94 ms per tick at 200 (0.94 reference cores), which the host can carry; the settlements cost 524 ms. At ×6 the integrated worst case needs 1.70 reference cores against its 1.0, and the world of 200 needs 10.04 against 4.8.
- **Recommendation.** Reverse the order of the remedy in ADR-080's confirmation: first the full-fidelity settlement cost (B1's population limit, the watched set, the overload lever of MB-2), then the host, and the battle size last, never below `pil.battle.bodies_typical` (400) without an owner decision. State in ADR-080 that the 2.1 µs basis is the whole-tick reading of DBG-205 and give the other reading's figures beside it. Ask gate B to confirm the caps as provisional ranges (population limit 450 to 750, battle cap 600 to 1,000) that the milestone measurement fixes, rather than as values, or bring the measurement forward (MB-3).

### A2-F03 — 10 Hz fails ADR-079's own latency confirmation once the client is counted, and the fallback invalidates the gate-B numbers

- **Severity:** High · **Category:** player experience
- **Where:** ADR-079 Consequences and Confirmation; ADR-084 item 4; ADR-087 items 4 and 5; Table 15; reference/06 R-CTRL-100 and table 2.2 rows D1 and D2 (the Strike team steered with the stick or the pointer, its lasers fired every tick).
- **Failure scenario.** At the first playable (O20), two players play Spawn Control: the host and a friend at a 50 ms round trip. The friend steers a Strike team with the stick (R-CTRL-100 row D1). Its control state is sent once per tick period (ADR-087 item 5) at a phase of its own, waits for the server's next tick, is executed, replicated, and then interpolated one tick plus 30 ms behind; nothing is predicted (ADR-084 item 6). The friend's stick moves the squad about 352 ms later on average and 422 ms at the 95th percentile, and lasers aimed at a figure that moves 20 wu/s land about 6 to 7 wu behind it. ADR-079's confirmation (p95 at most 250 ms on the host, at most 350 ms remote) fails for direct control on both clients, and for orders on the host it is slightly above its threshold (258 ms). The superseding ADR then moves to 20 ticks per second, which doubles the server CPU (the stage-3 host of A2-F01 then needs 38.6 ms per tick at ×1 against a wall budget of 25 ms), raises a battle view by 59 % and lifts the integrated host's upload for 15 remote stewards from 2.7 to 4.3 Mbit/s, above its 4 Mbit/s default: every gate-B number of ADR-080 and ADR-086 is then stale.
- **Numbers.** The model's means (187 ms host, 267 ms remote) omit the input sampling of the next frame (0 to 16.7 ms), the client's once-per-tick send of continuous control (0 to 100 ms), a queued frame and half a scan-out (25 ms), and the server step at 200 stewards (about 40 ms, A2-F01, not 20). The Monte Carlo with these terms (5 ms of exponential network jitter each way, which alone moves the model's remote mean from 267 to 277 ms; 200,000 samples) gives, mean / p95: host orders 212 / 258 ms; host direct control 262 / 331 ms; remote at 50 ms direct control 352 / 422 ms; remote at 150 ms 452 / 522 ms. At 20 Hz: 152 / 187 and 242 / 279 ms. The host leads a 50 ms remote player by 90 ms in direct control. Under loss, a lost tick update leaves the display 70 ms without its next state: in a battle view of 2 datagrams per tick, one hitch every 5.0 s at 1 % loss and every 0.5 s at the 10 % soak target, unless every datagram decodes on its own; at 10 Hz one lost update is 100 ms of motion.
- **Recommendation.** Keep 10 Hz only together with MB-1 (prediction of the steward's directly controlled units) and an immediate send of a control state that changed, instead of the once-per-period send; restate the latency model with the client terms and percentiles, and set the confirmation thresholds separately for orders and for direct control. Put a 20 Hz column beside every gate-B budget (CPU, wall time, host upload, egress), so that the owner confirms both and the fallback does not reopen gate B. Make every state datagram decodable on its own (records partitioned by datagram, each a delta against the same acknowledged baseline), so a loss costs only the figures in it.

### A2-F04 — Staggered checkpoints restore an inconsistent world after a crash, and an integrated host can duplicate an army

- **Severity:** High · **Category:** griefing or exploit
- **Where:** §4.3.1 items 1, 2 and 6; ADR-083 item 9; ADR-085 items 2 to 4; ADR-083 item 5 (id counters); DEC-251 option B ("consistent periodic snapshots and a journal of completed transactions") and item 5.
- **Failure scenario.** Each dirty owner checkpoints every 600 ticks on a phase fixed by its region id, and the world owner on a phase of its own; nothing records the cross-region messages between those moments. An integrated host sends an expedition of 300 over a bridge from region A to region B, waits until B has written its checkpoint (the store files' modification times show every owner's phase), and ends the client from the Windows Task Manager before A's next checkpoint: on reload A holds the expedition as it was before it left and B holds it after it arrived, so the host has 600. Without intent, a power cut during a dedicated world's evening peak restores a battle's region after the battle ended while the world owner's last checkpoint predates the result: the world owner holds the battle slot forever (the world's cap of WLD-285 shrinks by one per such crash), or applies a result twice (two grievances, two claim changes); and every stable id issued by a region after its checkpoint is issued again to a different object while another owner's later checkpoint still names the first.
- **Numbers.** Two uniform checkpoint phases over 60 s are on average 20 s apart. Counting battle starts, battle ends, attack declarations and border crossings (a third of attacks to a crossing neighbour, there and back), the expected number of cross-owner events inside that window is 0.062 per crash at 16 stewards and 0.615 at 200 at the evening peak: a crash leaves at least one inconsistency with a probability of 6.1 % at 16 and 45.9 % at 200 (0.9 % and 8.9 % over the day), and 100 % for a host who chooses the moment. A consistent cut is cheap: every owner's checkpoint at one tick is 1.4 MB at 16 and 12.7 MB at 200, 0.5 ms and 2.6 ms of serialisation on the host's workers once per 60 s.
- **Recommendation.** Replace the stagger by a consistent cut: at one tick boundary every dirty owner serialises into its buffer, after the delivery of the previous tick's messages, and the store commits the set as one transaction with a manifest that names the tick (or, if the stagger is kept for write smoothing, journal every cross-owner message durably and replay the messages after the newest checkpoint of each receiver at recovery, which Level 1 allows within one build). Persist the id counters in the same cut. Recovery loads the newest complete manifest. Add the deliberate-crash case to D9's threat model and to ADR-083's confirmation ("a forced kill at every phase of a crossing and of a battle end restores a world whose hashes equal a run that stopped at the manifest tick").

### A2-F05 — A second operating system cannot be built under AGENTS.md, and A2's own choices tie the server to Windows

- **Severity:** High · **Category:** technical feasibility
- **Where:** Table 1 row "Dedicated-server operating systems"; §4.3.1 item 8 ("which R14 allows and every server operating system can build"); ADR-083 item 1 ("portable C++23 from the first line"); ADR-081 items 2 and 6 (Level 2 "required for world generation"); ADR-088 item 4; ADR-058; `AGENTS.md` §1 to §3, R14 and R16; O21; vision §6 item 10.
- **Failure scenario.** Gate B follows the vision's recommendation of a Linux dedicated server, because most community hosts run Linux. The team then finds that the "portable" server library has never been compiled by a second compiler (AGENTS.md: toolset v145, `/std:c++latest`, no CMake, a Windows CI job only; ADR-051: no other compiler in the baseline; ADR-052: the portable subset is guidance, unenforced), and that A2's choices are Windows in-box services with no Linux counterpart that R14 allows: AES-GCM, ECDH and SHA-256 from CNG (ADR-088, ADR-060), XPRESS Huffman from the Compression API for backups and replays (§4.3.1 item 6, ADR-086 item 6), HTTPS through WinHTTP for D5's listing (ADR-077), crash dumps (ADR-065) and PIX markers (ADR-068). A world's backups and replays written on Windows cannot be read on Linux at all until an XPRESS Huffman decoder exists, so "a world moves by copying its records" fails for them.
- **What a Linux server build needs, each an owner decision at gate B:** (1) a compiler and standard library: GCC 14 or later with libstdc++, or Clang 18 or later with libc++, while R14 names only the MSVC standard library; (2) a build system: Visual Studio's MSBuild-based Linux projects, which keep MSBuild and the `.slnx` but use the GCC or Clang toolsets for WSL or a remote machine over SSH instead of v145 and `/std:c++latest` (Microsoft Learn, "Configure a Linux MSBuild C++ project in Visual Studio", https://learn.microsoft.com/cpp/linux/configure-a-linux-project; the same documentation recommends CMake for cross-platform work, https://learn.microsoft.com/cpp/build/walkthrough-build-debug-wsl2, which AGENTS.md forbids), or a hand-written build; (3) the R16 settings restated as GCC or Clang flags: `-std=c++23 -O2 -mavx2 -mfma -ffp-contract=off -fno-fast-math -Werror`, because GCC's documented default outside ISO C modes, C++ included, is `-ffp-contract=fast`, which contracts `a*b+c` into an FMA whenever the target has one, and Clang's is `on` within an expression; (4) cryptography: OpenSSL 3 (Apache-2.0) or libsodium (ISC), dynamically linked from the distribution or vendored, or an own X25519 and ChaCha20-Poly1305 implementation, which is security-critical new code; (5) an XPRESS Huffman codec, own code from the published [MS-XCA] specification (https://learn.microsoft.com/openspecs/windows_protocols/ms-xca/a8b7cb0a-92a6-4187-a23b-5e14273b96f8; my estimate: 1,000 to 2,000 lines with tests), or a change of codec for persisted data; (6) an HTTPS client for D5: libcurl with OpenSSL; (7) crash reporting and profiling replacements; (8) a Linux CI job that builds and tests the server, which `.github/workflows/build.yml` lacks; (9) a test that runs the Level 2 corpus on both builds. Items 1, 2, 4 and 6 are R14 or §3 exceptions; none is A2's to assume.
- **Recommendation.** Replace "every server operating system can build" and "portable from the first line" by what is true under O21: the server is a Windows build; the server library keeps Windows headers behind the platform layer so that a later port is a platform-layer port, not a rewrite. List items 1 to 9 as gate-B owner questions with their cost (A2's estimate, in engineer-weeks) and the alternative (Windows community servers, whose rental costs more than a Linux one). Make persisted data independent of Windows codecs now (store records uncompressed or with a codec the platform layer implements in plain C++), so that the port does not need a migration. If the owner wants Level 2 for the step to be real before a second build exists, add a compile-only clang-cl job (it uses the MSVC library, so it checks the compiler but not the library).

### A2-F06 — The region body budget can be filled by a rival, and the battle-size cap can make a full settlement unassailable

- **Severity:** High · **Category:** griefing or exploit
- **Where:** ADR-080 items 3, 4 and 9; WLD-281, WLD-285; O31 and PIL-015 ("no settlement becomes unassailable"); O2 (a defender's advantage, not a fortress).
- **Failure scenario 1, body-budget denial.** A full region holds 4 × 600 = 2,400 bodies of its settlements; 1,600 of the 4,000 remain. An attacker brings one battle of 1,000 figures to a settlement and parks 600 more at the battlefield's edge, where ADR-080 item 4 makes them wait. The region is at its body budget, so by item 9 "Essence waits at the Wellspring" for all four settlements of the region, the defender included: the defender cannot grow reinforcements while the attacker's waiting bodies hold the budget, and the three uninvolved settlements stop growing for the whole battle. At B1's maximum of 750 per settlement, the settlements take 3,000 and a single attacker of 1,000 fills the region.
- **Failure scenario 2, the cap as a wall.** The battle cap counts "figures of both sides together". If B4 counts the defender's Workers inside the battlefield of a battle at its settlement, a settlement at the population limit of 750 leaves 250 attacker places (3 defenders per attacker; 1.5 at 600), so a full settlement can be attacked only by forces it outnumbers three to one, which is the unassailable settlement that O31 rules out. If B4 does not count them, the cost model double counts nothing but the cap no longer bounds the battle's cost (750 defenders plus 1,000 others in one battlefield). At the 750 limit, the region's 1,000 remaining bodies also make the cap of 2 battles per region unreachable for battles of more than 500.
- **Recommendation.** Reserve each settlement's population limit inside the region body budget so that visitors can use only the 25 % share (1,000), with an expedition that would exceed it waiting in its own region or on its side of the bridge, and never count waiting attackers against the defender's growth. Define the battle cap per side (for example 500 per side, `wsim.battle.side_bodies_max`) and state which figures it counts, together with B4, and check the cap against the population limit for every value gate B may choose. Add both scenarios to D9's griefing list and to B6's protection model.

### A2-F07 — The model omits the seen layer, fidelity switches and an AI that is credible under fog

- **Severity:** Medium · **Category:** scale and cost
- **Where:** ADR-080 item 2 (region fixed cost 30 µs "the seen-layer summary"; AI 20 µs, "one 1 ms decision pass every 50 ticks"); ADR-086 item 1; PIL-057, PIL-084; WLD-244; reference/07 R-BOT-017 and table 2.1-A.
- **Failure scenario.** The reference has no fog of war: its bots read the whole state (R-BOT-017), so its cost basis contains no visibility computation. Species computes, for 256 settlements, what each sees (WLD-242) for every client's interest set every tick and for every AI and deputy. The model holds this in 30 µs per region. A grid stamp per body costs, at an assumed 0.25 to 1.0 µs per body per update (every tick for bodies in battle, every 5 ticks for the rest), +8.0 to +31.8 ms per tick at 200 (5 to 19 %) and +1.6 to +6.3 ms at 16 (6 to 22 %). A steward who zooms between the strategic and the settlement view flips up to 6 settlements between fidelities (PIL-057 counts only the settlement view), and nothing in A2's records gives the switch a hysteresis or a cost: at 2 to 5 µs to materialise each of 600 figures and a toggle every 10 to 30 s by every present steward, +2.3 to +16.9 ms per tick at 200. The reference's hard bot runs its main cycle every 11 ticks and its unit policies every tick; an AI at 100 or 200 µs per settlement per tick instead of 20 raises the 200 stress case to 187.8 or 213.4 ms.
- **Recommendation.** Add a seen-layer term per body per update, a switch cost and an AI range to the model, marked placeholder for D7 and D9. Give the fidelity switch a hysteresis in ADR-079 item 3 or ADR-085 item 6 (a settlement stays at full fidelity for at least `wsim.fidelity.hold_s`, provisional 60 s, after the last settlement view leaves it), which also narrows the abstract-versus-full exploit surface of seed challenge 7. Count these terms in A2-F01's wall time.

### A2-F08 — Bandwidth beyond the views in Table 9: skirmish, first join, baseline history and loss

- **Severity:** Medium · **Category:** scale and cost
- **Where:** ADR-086 items 2 to 5; ADR-088 item 2 (blob transfer); ADR-083 item 8 (terrain chunks by digest); Tables 9 and 12; DBG-204.
- **Failure scenario.** (1) Skirmish is the balance test bed (O8) at the design population of 2,000 units on a small map; a remote player's view of a fight at that population needs 48.0 KB/s at a one-tick baseline and 51.8 KB/s at a 150 ms round trip, and 65.6 KB/s at the stress population of 4,000, so both players of the first playable see part of every large fight at tier 2 (the in-process transport keeps the same budgets, ADR-084 item 1), in the mode whose purpose is to judge crowd combat. (2) A late joiner of a world of 200 receives its baked terrain (63.1 MB of binary64 heights, WLD-166) through a blob channel that has no budget of its own; at the per-client cap it takes 21.9 minutes, and 2.3 minutes for the 6.6 MB of a world of 16; ADR-086's "rejoin within 10 s" holds only with a full cache. (3) A delta against the last acknowledged state needs every sent-but-unacknowledged state per client: 32 ticks of history (3.2 s under 10 % loss) are 1.8 MB per client in a valley war, 84 MB at 47 clients and 358 MB at 200, against Table 12's 2.6 MB; this fits 2 GB, but the memory table understates it 30 to 140 times. (4) The 22-bit move of a tier-1 figure holds within 6 %: at a 150 ms round trip the baseline is two ticks old and a move needs 9 bits per axis (+4 to +6 % per view).
- **Recommendation.** Add a skirmish view at 2,000 and 4,000 units to the model and give skirmish its own cap (64 KB/s) or tier radius. Give blob transfers a budget of their own beside the state cap, send client terrain as 16-bit quantised heights compressed (12.1 MB at 200, 1.3 MB at 16) and stream it by level of detail, the strategic level first. Put the baseline history ring into the memory table. Keep the 22-bit figure and state its round-trip assumption.

### A2-F09 — Every simulation draw is predictable from a seed that the host is shown, and some draws collide

- **Severity:** Medium · **Category:** security
- **Where:** ADR-082 items 1 to 3 and 6; WLD-006 ("world seed ... drawn at creation, shown to the host"); WLD-161.
- **Failure scenario.** Every simulation draw is the keyed value of the world seed with keys that a client knows or sees: the domain, the purpose (an enumerator in the client's own code), the subject (a stable id that replication sends), the tick and the ordinal. A world host who shares the seed (to recreate the world shape on another server, which WLD-161 and AC-21 encourage) gives every steward the outcome of every Supply crate pick-up and every damage roll in advance. `Mix64` is a bijection, so one replicated raw 32-bit draw value (a cosmetic variant id, a seed passed to presentation) lets a client recover the hidden half with 2³² inversions, seconds on one GPU, and then the seed. Separately, every owner-less draw uses subject 0, so two regions that draw the same purpose in the same tick for a world event draw the same value; and a `SimRng` context made twice for one subject, purpose and tick (two code paths in two slices) restarts its ordinal at 0 and repeats its values.
- **Recommendation.** Key simulation draws by a 128-bit simulation key that is never shown or replicated, stored in the world's records and separate from the public world seed that generation uses (domain separation); use a keyed function that is not invertible from its output, for example SipHash-2-4 over the same keys (about 20 ns a draw, still well below 1 % of a tick at the reference draw rates). Use the owner's id as the subject of an owner-level draw. Make a second context for one (subject, purpose, tick) a Debug assertion. Never replicate a raw draw value.

### A2-F10 — Fog of war holds for remote clients only: the integrated host and the replays see everything

- **Severity:** Medium · **Category:** security
- **Where:** Table 5 row "Cheating and fair information"; ADR-084 Consequences ("fog of war holds by construction"); ADR-086 item 6; PIL-084; PIL-140.
- **Failure scenario.** (1) In stage 1 the server runs inside the host's own process (O10). A host who is also a steward reads every settlement's stores and every expedition from memory with a process memory scanner; the transport boundary protects the remote stewards' information from them but not from the host. (2) A battle replay is "the tier-1 stream of an observer who sees the whole battlefield", played to the steward who was away: the attacker's steward watches the defender's whole battlefield, including Workers, structures and reserves that its settlement never saw (PIL-084), and the defender's steward sees the attacker's full composition.
- **Recommendation.** State the trust model in §4.3: the integrated host is trusted with full information, which O33's default (attacks between stewards off in integrated worlds) already limits; D9 owns the wording players see. Record replays per side from that side's seen layer (two streams per battle: +1.19 GB of retained replays at 200 stewards, +121 MB at 16) or filter one stream on the server at playback by the viewer's seen layer at each tick.

### A2-F11 — An operator can edit the store and the record that should reveal the edit

- **Severity:** Medium · **Category:** security
- **Where:** §4.3.1 items 6 and 8; ADR-087 item 8; PIL-133; seed challenge 9.
- **Failure scenario.** A community operator who plays on the server stops it, edits the own-format record files to give an ally's settlement 2,000 more stores, deletes the "edited" entry that PIL-133 requires, and restarts. Every check A2 names runs inside the server the operator controls, so nothing is visible to stewards. A rollback to a backup is the same: "every restore is recorded" in a record that the restorer writes.
- **Recommendation.** Make tamper evidence independent of the server: every world-record entry and every checkpoint manifest extends a hash chain; each client stores the newest chain hash it was shown (32 bytes per world) and, on rejoin, requires a chain that extends it, so a rollback or an edit of anything the steward saw is detected and shown to that steward. Cost: one SHA-256 per appended entry and 32 bytes per client per world. State plainly in §4.3 what this can and cannot detect (an operator who edits before any client has seen a state remains undetectable).

### A2-F12 — Same-tick commands execute in arrival order, so the host wins every race

- **Severity:** Medium · **Category:** player experience
- **Where:** ADR-087 item 4; ADR-084 item 1 (in-process transport for the host).
- **Failure scenario.** Two stewards order an expedition to the same free site, or two players of a skirmish race for a Supply crate. Both commands reach the same tick; the host's arrives through the in-process queue half a round trip (25 ms at 50 ms) before the remote one, so it executes first every time and the remote command finds its target taken (a defined no-op, item 3). Combined with the 90 ms lead in direct control of A2-F03, the host of the first playable has a systematic edge in the game that is meant to test the balance.
- **Recommendation.** Order the commands of one tick by a key that does not depend on latency, for example `Mix64(tick, steward id)` rotated each tick, while each connection's own commands keep their order. Offer skirmish an optional host input delay equal to the median remote one.

### A2-F13 — Level 2 across compilers has hazards beyond floating point that ADR-081 does not list

- **Severity:** Medium · **Category:** testability
- **Where:** ADR-081 items 2 to 7 and its Confirmation; ADR-082; R-SIM-680.
- **Verification first.** A2's claim about MSVC is correct for the v145 toolset: since Visual Studio 2022, `/fp:precise` generates no floating-point contractions unless `/fp:contract` is given, and the `fp_contract` pragma defaults to off (Microsoft Learn: "`/fp` (Specify floating-point behavior)", https://learn.microsoft.com/cpp/build/reference/fp-specify-floating-point-behavior, and "`fp_contract` pragma", https://learn.microsoft.com/cpp/preprocessor/fp-contract). AGENTS.md R16's sentence that `/arch:AVX2` lets MSVC contract under `/fp:precise` describes compilers before Visual Studio 2022. The UCRT chooses FMA3 or non-FMA3 implementations of its transcendental functions at start-up, and their results can differ between computers (https://learn.microsoft.com/cpp/c-runtime-library/reference/get-fma3-enable-set-fma3-enable), so ADR-081 item 3's ban on CRT transcendentals is necessary. With contraction off in both compilers, x86-64 scalar SSE2 arithmetic gives the same correctly rounded binary64 results under MSVC and under GCC or Clang.
- **Failure scenario.** The cross-build test of a later Linux server fails, not on arithmetic but on the library and the data model: `std::sort`, `std::nth_element`, `std::partial_sort` and the heap functions order equal elements differently in the MSVC library and in libstdc++, so a target search that sorts candidates by distance picks a different target on a tie; `long` is 32 bits on Windows and 64 bits on Linux and `long double` is binary64 under MSVC and 80-bit extended under GCC on x86-64; `wchar_t` is 2 bytes against 4. ADR-081 would then fall back to its item 9 (fixed point), a rewrite, for causes that rules would have prevented.
- **Recommendation.** Add to ADR-081 item 2 and to the R18 candidates: simulation and record code uses fixed-width integer types only, no `long`, `long double` or `wchar_t`; every sort or selection whose result reaches an outcome is stable or uses a total order that ends with the stable id; no `std::hash` value reaches an outcome. Make `Build\CheckProjectFiles.py` or clang-tidy reject the types and flag `std::sort` in simulation projects. Note in ADR-081 that Level 2 between the client's integrated server and the Windows dedicated server holds by construction when both link one compiled server library, which is what world generation needs today.

### A2-F14 — Persistence across patches has no way back from a bad patch, and the crash safety of a home PC is unstated

- **Severity:** Medium · **Category:** operability
- **Where:** §4.3.1 items 1, 5, 6 and 8; DEC-251 items 5 and 6; O1 ("world state in a database"); seed challenge 11.
- **Failure scenario.** A patch migrates every record type of a world forward and contains a defect in the battle rules. The operator wants the previous build back: the previous server "refuses a world written by a newer schema", and the 7 daily backups taken after the migration are of the new schema too, so the only way back is a backup from before the patch, if one is still kept, losing every world day since. On a host's PC, "a record set is written whole or not at all" and "keeps the last two complete checkpoints" are stated without the means: write-through and flush order, atomic replacement of a file, detection of a torn or lost write, and what happens when the store's folder is redirected or synchronised by a cloud client.
- **Recommendation.** Before any migration, keep a pre-migration copy of the whole world for `per.migration.keep_days` (provisional 14 real days) and record the migration in the world record; let a server of the previous release open that copy; run every migration first on a copy and compare record counts and owner hashes before switching. State the write protocol (write a new file, flush it with write-through, then replace atomically on the same volume, with a checksum per record set) or use an engine that provides it (MB-4). Keep the store in the user-data folder of ADR-071 and warn the world host when that folder is synchronised by a cloud client. Say in §4.3.1 how the own store meets O1's "database" and DEC-251's "embedded engine", or adopt one.

### A2-F15 — Species designs its own encrypted handshake where Windows ships DTLS

- **Severity:** Medium · **Category:** security
- **Where:** ADR-088 item 4 and Consequences ("a security-sensitive part, which area D9 reviews"); seed challenge 12.
- **Failure scenario.** A small team writes an elliptic-curve key agreement, AES-GCM nonce management, replay windows, rekeying, a stateless handshake cookie against spoofed-source amplification, and certificate pinning by itself. One defect (a GCM nonce reused after a reconnect, or a handshake that answers a spoofed 60-byte datagram with a 1,200-byte reply) breaks the confidentiality of every connection or makes every community server a reflector. ADR-088 leaves the design of the handshake to a later review.
- **Recommendation.** Use the Schannel SSP's DTLS 1.2 through SSPI for `UdpTransport`: it is in-box on every supported Windows build (Windows 10 version 1607 and later, Windows 11, Windows Server 2016 and later; Microsoft Learn, "Protocols in TLS/SSL (Schannel SSP)", https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/secauthn/protocols-in-tls-ssl--schannel-ssp-), R14 allows it, it uses CNG, and Microsoft describes it as "reducing the need to use IPsec or designing a custom application layer security protocol" (https://learn.microsoft.com/windows-server/security/tls/datagram-transport-layer-security-protocol). Keep A2's own reliability, channels and fragmentation above it, and the trust-on-first-use pinning of a self-signed server certificate. If the owner keeps the own handshake, specify it in ADR-088 now (nonce construction, replay window, cookie, rekey interval) rather than after implementation.

### A2-F16 — Identity, listing and relay: what an R14 exception would buy, and what stage 1 needs without one

- **Severity:** Medium · **Category:** operability
- **Where:** Table 1 row "Online services"; ADR-086 item 5 (a returning steward reclaims the settlement "by the identity of area D5"); ADR-088 items 1 and 4, Option A and Consequences ("players hosting at home need area D5's rendezvous or relay to be reachable"); O5, O10.
- **Failure scenario.** The first world-game milestone runs an integrated world at the host's home with remote friends. A host behind carrier-grade NAT or a router without UPnP cannot be reached at all, because no relay exists until D5 provides one; and a returning friend reclaims its settlement from the deputy by an identity that no design yet defines, so anyone who joins under the same name could take it over.
- **The options, exactly** (the third-party SDKs as their vendors publicly document them; not verified in this round beyond the Microsoft pages cited).
  - **Steamworks SDK** (Valve; third-party, an R14 exception): authentication session tickets for identity and entitlement, the server browser and lobbies for listing, and Steam Datagram Relay through its networking sockets for relay and NAT traversal; no fee beyond Steam distribution; a Linux game-server library exists. It ties identity and reachability to one storefront (ADR-072).
  - **Epic Online Services SDK** (Epic; third-party, an R14 exception): Connect for identity across storefronts, Sessions and Lobbies for listing, and P2P with relays; no fee; storefront-neutral; Linux supported.
  - **PlayFab Party with PlayFab Lobby** (Microsoft, distributed as the NuGet package `Microsoft.PlayFab.PlayFabParty.Cpp.Windows`; Microsoft Learn, "PlayFab Party SDKs", https://learn.microsoft.com/xbox/playfab/multiplayer/networking/party-sdks): relayed networking and lobbies with PlayFab identity; a Microsoft NuGet package like the three of R14, but not on the list, and a metered service.
  - **Without an exception:** identity as a key pair that the client generates with CNG (ECDSA P-256) and each server registers on first join (no service, no cross-server identity, key backup is the player's); listing as a small HTTPS master server that someone operates before "official hosting later" (O5); reachability through UPnP port mapping with the in-box NAT API (`natupnp.h`, `Hnetcfg.dll`; Microsoft Learn, https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/api/natupnp/nn-natupnp-iupnpnat), IPv6 and manual port forwarding, with hosts behind carrier-grade NAT left unreachable; or an own UDP relay, whose egress for one relayed world of 15 remote stewards in battle views is 2.7 Mbit/s, 4.9 GB per 4-hour session.
- **Recommendation.** Write these four options, with the numbers above, as one gate-B owner question, recommending the R14-clean baseline (key-pair identity, UPnP and IPv6, manual address) for the first world-game milestone and a relay as a third `Transport` implementation once D5 and the storefront decision (ADR-072) choose its provider. Make the key-pair identity part of ADR-088's handshake now, since ADR-086's reclaim depends on it from stage 1.

### A2-F17 — The client's composition root can reach the server's state, so separability is a convention there

- **Severity:** Medium · **Category:** consistency
- **Where:** §4.2.2 ("the client links game presentation, the client session and the server library, and composes the integrated server as a child of its root"); ADR-083 item 2; ADR-084 item 2; O10; seed challenge 1.
- **Failure scenario.** The include allow-lists bind the client session and the game presentation libraries, but the client executable's own project, which holds the composition root, includes the server library's headers to compose it. A developer adds a debug overlay or a "host tools" panel to that project, reading region owners directly because it is convenient; the build passes and the integrated host now draws from server state that a remote client cannot have, which is exactly the divergence path O10 forbids.
- **Recommendation.** Put the integrated server's composition behind one header of the server library (for example `IntegratedServer.h`, exposing start, stop and a `Transport` endpoint, nothing else), and make the checker allow the client executable that header only. Route every host tool and diagnostic through the administration interface of ADR-088 item 7, as remote operators use it.

### A2-F18 — Ledger and model consistency

- **Severity:** Low · **Category:** consistency
- **Where:** ledger 12 rows for §2.3, §2.4, §2.7, R-SIM-581, R-SIM-583, R-SIM-605, R-SIM-606, R-SIM-672, R-SIM-740, R-SIM-741; §2.2 and §2.5 rows; ADR-083 item 7; `A2ServerBudget.py`.
- **Findings.** (1) The rows mark as `drop` requirements whose behaviour Species keeps in another form and names an ADR as the replacement; `drop` means "not in Species" (prompt §5.3) and `adapt` needs Species requirements, so the ledger totals count adaptations as drops until D2 converts them, and nothing tracks that conversion. (2) The §2.2 row adopts R-SIM-510, which defines a tick as the batch "that the tick source sequences", while §2.3 drops the sequencer. (3) The §2.5 row keeps skirmish game speed "as tick scheduling on the server's tick loop (ADR-079)", but neither ADR-079 nor ADR-083 item 7 says how many world ticks a period runs at a speed of 0.2 to 2.0 (R-SIM-548, R-SIM-549, DEC-264), and at Time Rush the skirmish server's CPU and, per world tick, its bandwidth double for 151 ticks. (4) The model's docstring of `latency_ms` says half a frame while the code adds a whole frame; the hash term divides by `tick_hz` where ADR-081 item 8 says once every 10 ticks, which is right only at 10 Hz. (5) PIL-140 asks for the deputy's decisions on the replay timeline; ADR-086 item 6 records only state.
- **Recommendation.** Write the replacing requirements now (an SRV or WSIM requirement per dropped section) or use `adapt` rows that cite the ADR items, as the checker allows; state game-speed scheduling in ADR-083 item 7; fix the two model details; add a decision track to the replay stream.

## Make it better

### MB-1 — Predict only the steward's directly controlled units, and keep 10 Hz

The client already links the server library (§4.2.2). For the one Strike team, Builder, Collector or turret that the steward controls directly, the client runs that unit's movement with the server's own code from the latest replicated state plus the unsent and unacknowledged control states, draws the predicted position, and blends to the server's state when it arrives (correction over 100 ms). Nothing else is predicted: crowds stay interpolated, and every effect (a hit, a pick-up) stays server-side. **Benefit:** direct-control movement responds within one frame on every client, which removes the main reason for 20 Hz (A2-F03) and keeps every tick-denominated reference value, the CPU and the bandwidth budgets as they are. **Cost:** one prediction and reconciliation path for the 3 directly steered moving units (Strike team, Builder, Collector) and local turret aim, Level 1 determinism of their movement code inside one build (already required), and a test that compares the predicted and server paths; estimated 2 to 3 engineer-weeks.

### MB-2 — An overload lever that spares battles: neighbour-only watched settlements at abstract fidelity

Of the 177 settlements watched at the 200 stress peak, about 130 (177 less the 47 present stewards' own) are watched only because they lie within 800 wu of a present steward's own territory (WLD-244: 6 at most per present steward, 47 present). When the tick wall time exceeds its budget, the server demotes those neighbour-only settlements, least recently viewed first, to abstract fidelity with a presentation stand-in (their Workers drawn from the abstract record's tasks, at tier-2 rates), and promotes them back when the load falls; a steward's own settlement and every battle never degrade. **Benefit:** up to 96 of the 167 ms per tick at 200 (57 %), so the world survives costs of ×2 to ×3 on the 8-vCPU host without shrinking battles (A2-F02) or slowing world time (A2-F01). **Cost:** an amendment of PIL-057 and WLD-244 (A0 and A1, owner gate), a stand-in presentation (C3 and the renderer), and B2's accuracy target must then hold for watched neighbours as well.

### MB-3 — Measure before gate B fixes the caps

Build, before gate B, a one-week cost spike: a headless C++ program under the AGENTS.md settings with the reference target search, movement and laser bolts for 2,000 figures in one region on a grid, measured on the developer machine and on a 4-core AVX2 minimum-class CPU. **Benefit:** gate B confirms the population limit, the battle size and the host specifications on a measured µs per figure instead of a guidance figure with two readings (A2-F02), and the ×1.14 to ×1.30 margin of A2-F01 becomes a known number. **Cost:** about one engineer-week, thrown away or kept as the first benchmark of the simulation-core milestone (DEC-229).

### MB-4 — Use the in-box Extensible Storage Engine for the stage-1 and stage-2 record store

ESE (ESENT) is a Windows component with ACID transactions over a write-ahead log, crash recovery at start-up and online backup, reached through `esent.h` and `esent.lib` of the Windows SDK and `esent.dll` in every Windows (Microsoft Learn, "Extensible Storage Engine", https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/extensible-storage-engine/extensible-storage-engine). R14 allows it, and O21 already makes the server a Windows build until a later ADR. Each owner's record set becomes rows of one table keyed by owner and record type; a consistent checkpoint (A2-F04) is one transaction; backups use the engine's online backup. **Benefit:** the crash safety, torn-write detection and recovery that §4.3.1 needs, without writing a journaled format, and an embedded engine as DEC-251 option B recommends and O1's "database" reads. **Cost:** an ESE dependency of the platform layer's store implementation, its file-set handling (logs, checkpoint file) in the install layout of ADR-071, and a port of the store interface for any later non-Windows server (which A2-F05 already requires).

### MB-5 — One in-box security stack for the transport and identity

Combine the Schannel DTLS 1.2 transport security of A2-F15 with the key-pair identity of A2-F16: the client's long-lived identity key (ECDSA P-256 in CNG, stored in the user's key store) signs, inside the established DTLS session, a server-chosen nonce together with the server certificate's fingerprint, and the client pins that certificate on first use. **Benefit:** connection security, server authentication and player identity for stages 1 and 2 with no service, no R14 exception and no own cryptographic protocol; a storefront identity can be bound to the same key later. **Cost:** SSPI integration (Schannel's API is verbose), a certificate per server generated at first start, and a key backup and transfer flow for players (C2).
