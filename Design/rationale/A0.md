# Rationale A0: vision, pillars and core loops

> Status: Final · Area: A0 · Document: [Vision, pillars and core loops](../gdd/00_vision_pillars_and_core_loops.md) · Ledger: [00 reference overview](../gdd/adoption_ledger/00_reference_overview_ledger.md) · Related: [owner decisions](../decisions/owner_decisions.md) O31 to O37 · [rationale index](README.md)

This file keeps the reasons behind the A0 design: the weakest point that the challenge found, every finding and how it was resolved, the alternatives that were rejected, the recommendations, and the owner decisions taken at gate A. One challenge round was run (23 findings: 1 Critical, 5 High, 12 Medium, 5 Low; 6 improvements).

## Weakest point

Every target of the first draft was written in world time, which passes only while a server runs, and the draft never worked out what that meant in the stage that ships first. In stage 1 the server is integrated in the host's client (O10) and the world freezes when the client closes (O4), so for a solo steward or the host of a friends' world, world time is roughly play time. At the draft's targets a solo steward would meet one battle every 16.8 to 84 hours of play and reach the settlement plateau after 336 hours of play, and the away loop (the deputy, the protection rules, the away report) would never run between sessions. The fix keeps O4 and O10 as written: two world paces (PIL-100 to PIL-104), with the session pace running the economy, relations and protection timers 12 times faster, and an always-on integrated world that keeps running while the host's client stays open with its presentation suspended (PIL-077; O35).

## Alternatives rejected

| Choice | Rejected | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Pillar set | The vision's five as written; merging pillars 2 and 4 | The five left literal contradictions with O4 and O9 and no pillar on runaway leaders, late joiners or ruinous losses; a merged safe-absence pillar loses the separate test of the deputy's trustworthiness |
| Pillar 2 name | "A world that keeps running"; "Safe to be away" | The first contradicts O4 when read literally; the second drops the persistent world |
| Pillar 5 name | "The reference look and feel"; "One look, read from afar" | The first reads as copying the reference look; the second drops the newness that O9 requires |
| Protection period name | Shield, Ward, Sanctuary, Aegis | Shield is already a Supply crate outcome; the others are item or place names of well-known games |
| Settlement centre name | Hearth, Nexus, Core | Associated with well-known game trademarks, or colliding with the Core Device |
| Settlement player name | owner, ruler, founder, keeper, warden | "owner" already names the product owner and a match's owning team; the others are wrong after a hand-over or are known game titles and character classes |
| Name of the peoples | race; kin; the word Species | The owner chose Cladan (O32): race carries real-world weight when peoples differ in colour and grow angry at each other; kin reads as family; the title collides with the game and its namespace |
| Force sent out | warband, host, column, raid | Part of a known game title, the server host, a table word, or too narrow (an expedition also reinforces and gathers) |
| Server runner | operator, admin | operator is the Worker in a port; admin fits only a dedicated server |
| Ledger treatment of restatements | Adopt for both forms; drop as a summary | Adopt would bind the world to rules whose owners had not decided; drop would remove the skirmish summary that tests cite |
| Typical battle length | 2 to 5 minutes; 15 to 30 minutes | Too short for crowd play, or longer than a check-in and than most sessions allow |
| Presence never lowers protection (first PIL-086) | Withdrawn | It forbade the natural counter to attacks on the away (A0-F04) |
| An away-yield band of 50 to 80 % as the guard (first PIL-023) | Replaced | Met exactly, it made a 12-fold difference in play worth 2 to 8 % of growth (A0-F03) |
| Stage-1 world after the game closes, as a background headless process (A0-F01, option B) | Not chosen (O35) | It amends O4's example and adds a local dedicated form to stage 1; the open client with its presentation suspended gives the player the same result |
| The session pace only in stage 1 (A0-F01, option C) | Not chosen (O35) | Stage 1 would ship without absent play |
| Attacks only with a cause; no attacks between stewards in v1; no surcharge (Q3) | Not chosen (O33) | Steward play would depend on AI grievances; conflict between players would be lost; a steward's attack could be random |
| Removal never at all; removal by battle (Q4) | Not chosen (O34) | Worlds fill with deputy-run settlements; the eight-hour sleeper could wake to a refounding |
| Essence uncollectable between grievance-free stewards (A0-F05) | Not used | Every declared attack records a grievance, so the condition never holds; the transfer bound covers feeding |
| Full fidelity only in stage 1 (A0-F17) | Not used | O3 makes the abstract model an owner decision; a deferral can be proposed at gate B with its cost |

## Recommendations

**Adopted:** a sixth pillar (O31); measurable guards for every pillar (§2.3); keeping a stage-1 world running in the open client (O35); respite for the shield period, steward for the settlement's player and world host for the server's runner (O37); the presence-gain guard (PIL-023); AI attacks only with a grievance or claim on record and a warning for every attack (PIL-025); a source of Essence that needs no death (PIL-056); claims (PIL-120); dormancy (O34); the check-in entry (PIL-141); replays and renown as SHOULD rules (PIL-140, PIL-125); skirmish as the battle trainer (PIL-032); a registry search of the vocabulary and the product name before release.

**Rejected or withdrawn:** presence never lowering protection (withdrawn for PIL-086); the away-yield band (replaced); trade between stewards (left to B2); defence windows as a mandate (one lever of B6). **Passed on:** a weekly world digest for the days-and-weeks loop, recommended to C3 and B5.

## Model results

None required for A0. The challenger's session-pacing check, a script over the draft's tuning values, gave the numbers behind A0-F01, A0-F03, A0-F11 and A0-F13: a steward is present for 0.7 % (10 minutes a day) to 16.7 % (240 minutes a day) of an always-on world's time; under a 50 to 80 % away yield, 120 minutes a day out-grew 10 minutes a day by only 7.6 to 1.9 %; in an integrated world whose host plays 2 hours a day the 14-day plateau took 168 real days, which the session factor of 12 brings to 14; and at an assumed churn of half the stewards a month, a 16-steward world would hold 24 abandoned settlements after 3 months without dormancy. B2, B5 and B6 build the models that confirm the provisional targets.
