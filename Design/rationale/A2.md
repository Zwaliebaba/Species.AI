# Rationale A2: solution strategy

> Status: Final · Area: A2 · Documents: [solution strategy](../architecture/04_solution_strategy.md) and the records ADR-079 to ADR-089 ([ADR index](../ADR/README.md)) · Ledger: [12 simulation model](../gdd/adoption_ledger/12_simulation_model_ledger.md) (sections 01 and 07) · Model: `Tools/DesignModels/A2ServerBudget.py` · Related: [owner decisions](../decisions/owner_decisions.md) O41 to O49 · [rationale index](README.md)

This file keeps the reasons behind the A2 design: the weakest point that the challenge found, every finding and how it was resolved, the alternatives that were rejected, the recommendations, the model results and the owner decisions taken at gate B. One challenge round was run (18 findings: 0 Critical, 6 High, 11 Medium, 1 Low; 5 improvements); the proposer accepted 16 findings and escalated 2, and gate B (2026-10-18, O41 to O49) answered the escalations and the owner questions. The answers were applied at Finalise on 2026-10-19, after the owner had paused the design phase and added the MVP (O50 to O55), so the records' "Confirmed when" lines now name the MVP's milestones.

## Weakest point

The draft's budgets looked about three times roomier than they were. It sized the stage-3 host by average cores, so a world of 200 at its stress case looked like 35 % of an 8-vCPU host, but its own 50 ms tick wall budget and single-threaded region steps made that host miss the budget at 1.14 to 1.30 times the per-figure costs, which are unmeasured targets read from a guidance figure that can also be read as six times higher (DBG-205). Both likely corrections broke the plan that gate B was to confirm: a cost overrun, whose stated remedy shrank battles although watched settlements were most of the cost, and a move to 20 ticks per second, which the draft's own latency confirmation was likely to trigger once the client's terms were counted, doubling the CPU and lifting the integrated host's upload above its default. The revision answered it with the tick's wall time and a margin rule (twice the cost budgets within the wall budget, four times within the deadline), which put stage 3 on 16 vCPU (×2.22) and stage 2 on 4 vCPU (×2.51); a remedy order that shrinks battles last; the prediction of the directly controlled unit, which keeps 10 Hz; the 20 Hz figures beside every budget; and the owner's choice at gate B to keep the budgets provisional with ranges until the first task of the simulation-core milestone, now the MVP's M1, measures the costs (O43).

## Alternatives rejected

| Choice | Rejected | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Tick rate (DEC-227) | 20 or 30 ticks per second; a higher full rate with a lower abstract rate | Every tick value of every reference table would be converted, so skirmish would no longer test the reference rules; twice or three times the CPU (3.71 reference cores at 200 against 1.85) and 59 % more bandwidth in a battle view; prediction gives direct control its latency without it |
| Budgets (DEC-228) | A client-derived budget alone; a server-first budget alone; no hard budget | Alone each leaves one host unbudgeted; without hard caps battle outcomes would depend on the host's hardware |
| Host sizing | Average cores (the draft); 8 vCPU at stage 3 with region hosts or the lever from the first overrun | A tick is a wall-time budget; 8 vCPU reaches it at ×1.02 the costs (O48 chose 16 vCPU) |
| Region bodies | A shared pool of 4,000 bodies; a total battle cap of 1,000 | A rival's waiting bodies could stop a region's growth, and a defender of 750 could not be met by an equal force (A2-F06) |
| World battle cap | A1's max(4, 10 %) | Attacks wait during 4 % of the evening peak at 16 (11 % at the session pace); the owner chose max(8, 10 %) (O46) |
| Numeric model (DEC-240) | The reference model; fixed point; one build only | The CRT's CPU-dependent functions break even Level 1 across computers; fixed point rewrites the engine baseline for a guarantee needed only for world generation and diagnostics |
| Random draws (DEC-242) | One sequential stream; a stream per system; `Mix64` over the public seed (the draft) | A global draw order across parallel regions; a seed shown to the host makes every draw predictable (A2-F09) |
| Process architecture (DEC-221 to DEC-226) | A service registry with a full job graph; the reference structure | Order-dependent rules become hard to verify; the reference contradicts the headless server and O10 |
| Client boundary (DEC-222) | Shared read-only views; the reference's direct access and extrapolation | Neither serves a remote client nor enforces fog of war |
| Region partitioning | One world owner; overlapping region margins | No parallel or distributed regions without a rewrite; two owners of one area must agree bit for bit |
| Replication (DEC-200) | A command-stream model for skirmish; client-side re-simulation | Two netcodes, Level 2 on every client and O8 changed |
| Replays | A command stream from the cut before the battle | Valid on one release only and under Level 2; the state stream costs about 10 times the storage and survives patches |
| Commands (DEC-202, DEC-203) | Arrival checks only; partial trust; execution in arrival order | A stale target has no defined result; a trusted path is a cheat path; the host would win every same-tick race |
| Transport (DEC-207) | A platform transport; Species' own transport over Schannel DTLS 1.2 (A2's round-1 proposal); a stream and a separate datagram channel | A platform service and a library outside R14; the owner chose MsQuic, which gives streams, datagrams and TLS 1.3 with no reliability layer of Species' own (O44); two transports double the code |
| Persistence approach | A single save file; an event-sourced world | O1 rules out the file; event sourcing needs Level 3 |
| Store technology | The in-box ESE (A2's recommendation for a Windows-only server); an embedded database library as an R14 exception | The owner chose a portable store with no exception (O42) |
| Second server operating system | A dedicated server in stage 2 for the operating system that most community hosts rent, as an `AGENTS.md` change (7 to 11 engineer-weeks); a deferral to stage 3 | The owner kept one Windows build with a portable core, a second system a later ADR (O41) |
| Identity, listing and relay | The Steamworks SDK, Epic Online Services, PlayFab Party, an own relay now | Each an R14 exception or a service to run before the storefront is chosen (O45, O49) |
| Overload | No lever, so that an overload slows world time | The owner approved the lever, which spares battles and every steward's own settlement (O47) |

## Recommendations

**Adopted:** a world battle cap of max(8, 10 %), 8 at 16 stewards (O46); one server process per world in stage 3 on 16 vCPU, with region hosts as the reserve beyond ×2.2 (O48); battle replays as recorded state streams with side masks and a decision track; the prediction of the directly controlled unit (MB-1); consistent cuts every 60 s, every 10 s in integrated worlds during battles; a command journal of the last 10 minutes with the cut before it on every server; a stall of 10 s or more recorded as a freeze; the overload lever (MB-2, O47); the R14-clean identity baseline (MB-5's identity part, O45).

**Rejected or passed on:** measuring before gate B (MB-3; the owner chose ranges that M1 fixes, O43); the ESE store engine (MB-4; the owner chose Species' own store, O42); Species' own transport over Schannel DTLS 1.2 (MB-5's transport part; the owner chose MsQuic, O44). Passed to owner gate C, which waits while the design phase is paused (O51): the amendment of `AGENTS.md` R16's FMA sentence (MSVC generates no contraction under `/fp:precise` since Visual Studio 2022, checked on Microsoft Learn) and the R18 candidates of the deterministic core (no CRT transcendental function in simulation code, keyed draws only, no floating-point pragma or `/fp:contract`, no operating-system header outside the platform layer, fixed-width types, stable or total orders, no `std::hash` or pointer value in an outcome, owners hold all mutable simulation state).

## Model results

`Tools/DesignModels/A2ServerBudget.py` is the record of every figure of the solution strategy's §4.5: `python3 -I Tools/DesignModels/A2ServerBudget.py check` compares 33 figures and 12 tables and reports 0 differences. Its CPU figures are cost budgets, not measurements; the MVP's M1 measures them (O43). At gate B the datagram overhead changed from the 77 bytes of the DTLS design to 89 bytes on IPv6 (69 on IPv4), computed from the framing of RFC 9000 and RFC 9221 with MsQuic's 9-byte connection ids and 4-byte packet numbers, which raised each view by 1 % to 2 %; M4 measures it (ADR-088 item 3).
