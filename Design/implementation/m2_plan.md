## 1 Purpose

This plan turns milestone M2 of the [MVP scope](mvp_scope.md), the settlement, into work an implementing session can start cold. It covers:
- what M2 owes, beyond the scope's own row (§2);
- how the first guesses of the scope's table 3 stand against M2's exit, before any code (§3);
- the order of the work (§4);
- the scripted steward and the growth measurement (§5, O80);
- the tasks of M2, each one or more changes on M2's pull request, as M1's were (§6, O71);
- the world the steward plays at M2 (§7);
- what is carried, open or interim at M2 (§8);
- the questions the owner answers before the plan is Final (§9);
- what the owner does during M2 (§10).

How work flows between the cloud session and the owner's PC is unchanged from the [M0 plan](m0_plan.md) §2: CI is the compiler, the owner runs what touches presentation, and nothing is claimed that was not done.

M2 ends when the exit criteria of the MVP scope's §3 hold:
- one settlement with no neighbours grows from 20 Workers to its plateau, with a growth step at least every `pil.settlement.growth_step_minutes` (20 minutes) of play in its first 5 hours, and plateaus after 4 to 6 hours of play;
- the steward finds it nice to watch for 10 minutes.

By O80, the scripted steward of §5 tunes the rules to the first criterion headless, and the owner's own play confirms it, measured by the server's statistics (MVP-064, pulled forward to M2 by the scope's amendment of table 2).

## 3 The first guesses against the exit

The scope's table 3 sets its first guesses against a plateau near 280 Workers paid for by the upkeep. A rough estimate of the rules of MVP-020 to MVP-038, made while this plan was written, suggests that they miss M2's exit by a wide margin, and that the plateau they reach is set by `mvp.settlement.population_max` rather than by the upkeep. It is a one-second-step model of one settlement with a greedy steward (a dwelling queued five Workers short of the limit, two storehouses once the outer resource sites are reached, orbs whenever the stores allow), the Worker's 20 wu/s over the valley path factor, a gathering cycle of the port's 10 s and the walk to the nearest drop-off and back, and no walking contention, port competition or pathing. Its figures are good to about a third; they say where the tuning starts, not where it ends.

- **The first hours are bound by 12 ports.** The founding territory of 500 wu holds only the three inner home resource sites at 350 wu (WLD-082), 12 gathering ports, so the income stays near 900 ru an hour while the population climbs to the outer ring.
- **The outer ring opens late.** The five outer sites at 800 wu enter the territory at about 95 Workers (`mvp.territory.radius_per_worker` of 4 wu), which the estimate reaches after about four hours of play.
- **The gaps between growth steps are too long early.** Each time the population meets its limit it waits for the next dwelling at the slow income: counting a completed structure or each further 25 Workers as a step, the largest gap in the first 5 hours is about 90 minutes with the first guesses, and about 50 minutes even when the territory grows twice as fast.
- **The plateau comes after the run.** The population reaches 280 at about 8.5 hours of play and the cap of 300 at about 9.5, against an exit of 4 to 6 hours and a run of 8.
- **The upkeep never binds.** At the plateau the 44 ports bring about 3,500 ru an hour with storehouses near the outer sites, against an upkeep of about 2,100 for 300 Workers and 11 structures, so growth stops at the cap, not where the income and the upkeep meet.

With `mvp.territory.radius_per_worker` at 6 or 8 the estimate reaches 280 Workers at about 6 hours and the cap at about 7, but the early gaps stay above 20 minutes. Whether they meet the exit depends on what counts as a growth step (§9, question 1). Two consequences follow for this plan. The tuning is a task of its own (T10), run on the scripted steward with stated levers in a stated order before any presentation is built around a settlement of that shape (§9, question 2). And M3 inherits the result: first contact between neighbours is timed by the same territory growth (the scope's §4 places it at about 150 Workers, in hour 2 to 3), so the M3 plan cannot be finished before T10 has run ([M3 plan](m3_plan.md) §3).

## 4 The order of M2

**The settlement comes first, headless and tuned, and the look follows.** As at M1, a measurement moves values that the later work is built around: here the shape of a settlement over a run, which sets the HUD's figures, how many structures the view draws and when contact arrives at M3. So the tasks fall in two phases, as M1's did:
- **Phase A, T2 to T10**, builds the settlement as a headless simulation. CI tests every task, and none needs a run checklist except T2, whose silhouettes the owner approves. It ends with T10, the scripted steward's growth scene and the tuning, and with the owner's run of the performance script on `Release|ARM64`.
- **Phase B, T11 to T19**, makes the settlement visible and playable. Every task states a run checklist (M0 plan §2) and is not merged before the owner's answer.

T2 and T3 come first because the simulation reads the structures' ports and footprints from the cooked simulation data records (AST-043, AST-046), never from a render mesh (ADR-021); until they land, T4 and T5 test on records written by the tests. T14 and T15, the UI framework and the settings store, depend on nothing in phase A and may run beside it, as M1's content build did (O73), each pushed with its run checklist; the HUD, the verbs and the front end (T16 to T18) follow T10, so that the tuning settles the figures they show.

## 5 The scripted steward and the growth measurement

O80 has a scripted steward tune M2 and the owner's play confirm it.

**The scripted steward is the economy half of an AI settlement.** It is written in GameLogic as a controller that reads only its own settlement and the public layer, sends its commands as an AI sender through the one command path of ADR-087, executed at the next tick (MVP-094, DEC-122), and holds its state in the snapshot, so that M3's AI settlements (MVP-056) extend it rather than start again. Its policy is fixed and stated in its task: the default priorities of MVP-036, a dwelling queued when the population comes within a stated margin of its limit, a storehouse placed between outer resource sites once the territory holds them, and a placement layout that keeps every port and path clear. It never reads anything the steward's client could not see.

**The growth scene** runs in `Tools/SpeciesPerformance` on the game's libraries, as M1's headless scenes do (ADR-110): one settlement at the steward's site with no neighbours, from a fixed seed and the scripted steward, for `mvp.run.length_hours` of play, stepped as fast as the machine allows. It reports, from the server's statistics (MVP-064):
- every growth step with its tick, and the largest gap between two of them in the first 5 hours;
- the time at which the plateau is reached, by the definition the task states (no growth step and a Worker count within a stated share of its maximum for an hour of play);
- the Worker count, the stores, the population limit and the strength (MVP-038) every `mvp.stats.sample_minutes`;
- the share of Workers idle by each reason of MVP-037.

It runs on a corpus of seeds, because every site has the same distances (WLD-042, WLD-082) but not the same slopes or paths. **In CI** a shortened scene of the first hour of play runs as a regression: its per-tick hashes are stable and its growth steps fall inside the bounds the task records. **On the reference test machine** the performance script runs the whole scene and writes its figures into `playtests/M2.md`.

**The owner's confirmation** is the owner's own play from 20 Workers to the plateau, across sessions that save and continue (MVP-075), with the same statistics recorded by the server and compared with the scene's.

## 7 The world of M2

M2's world is the MVP's valley with one settlement in it: the steward's, at the site the steward picks on the front end (MVP-012), from 20 Workers (MVP-020). The other three sites are empty, so the three AI settlements, contact with a neighbour, grievances, claims and attacks are M3's (MVP-027's contact is built and tested at M2 on a fixture). The world runs at the session pace on the integrated server (MVP-070), saves every `mvp.persist.snapshot_seconds` and when the client closes, and freezes while the client is closed (O4). Guard duty mans the turrets and gathers Workers near the Wellspring, and in M2 neither has anyone to fight; the turret's fire is tested headless against the sandbox's side (T5). The battle sandbox of the [M1 plan](m1_plan.md) §6 stays as a Debug build's start-up option, for the battle tuning that M3 needs.

## 8 Carried, open and interim at M2

- **The first guesses.** §3's estimate is not a measurement and is not cited as one; T10's growth scene is the measurement, and every value it moves is changed in the scope's table 3 and the tuning record, never in code (MVP-003).
- **Values outside the MVP's keys.** The founding radius, the territory's largest radius and the resource sites' places are values of the Final world design (WLD-060, WLD-082); T10 does not move them, and a need to goes to the owner (§9, question 2).
- **The seen layer** is built at M2 or at M3 as §9 question 4 decides. Either way the replication's tests against it (ADR-084, ADR-086) and the AI's reading of it (MVP-058) are M3's, and the AI reads the whole battle until then (O70).
- **Snapshots are whole worlds.** T9 measures the cost at a settlement's plateau; the incremental cut of ADR-083 item 9 comes with O42's store (ADR-099).
- **Saved versions.** M1 never saves, so no M1 world needs migrating. T9's ADR states the first saved version, whether a version raised during M2's development may refuse its predecessors with a message, and from which build every raise carries its migration (MVP-095).
- **No demolition.** MVP-101 gives the steward no demolish verb, although MVP-073 names demolition among the deputy's refusals; M2 builds none.
- **Numerics.** Simulation state stays binary64 (ADR-081) and the cooked records' binary32 socket transforms are widened on load; one build reproduces a run, and an ARM64 and an x64 build give different hashes, as at M1.
- **CI builds `Debug|x64` only** (`AGENTS.md` §6). The `Release|ARM64` runs of T10 and T19 are the owner's, as is a Release build of any change that could break only under optimisation.

## 9 Questions for the owner

Each question gives the options and the one this plan recommends; the answers are recorded as an owner decision and the plan becomes Final with them.

1. **What counts as a growth step.** PIL-055 names "a finished structure, a population milestone or a gain of territory", but the territory grows a little with every Worker (MVP-026), so a gain of territory taken literally makes the criterion empty. Options: (a) a structure completed, the population reaching each multiple of a step held in data, or the territory taking in a resource site it did not hold (recommended: each is a moment the steward sees); (b) a structure completed or the population limit raised, nothing else; (c) PIL-055 as written, with a gain of territory counted at each further 100 wu of radius.
2. **How T10 tunes.** Options: (a) only the `mvp.*` keys move, in this order: `mvp.territory.radius_per_worker` (when the outer ring opens), `mvp.cost.dwelling_ru` and `mvp.cost.dwelling_escalation` (the wait at each limit), `mvp.essence.orb_cost_ru` (the rate of growth), the upkeep keys (so that the plateau is where income and upkeep meet, below `mvp.settlement.population_max`), and last `mvp.resource.ports_per_unit`, which changes the resource-site meshes; a value of the world design goes to the owner (recommended); (b) the same, with `wld.territory.founding_radius` allowed to move as the first lever; (c) keep the values and restate the exit's targets from the growth scene's result.
3. **Who makes the structure meshes.** Options: (a) the mesh kit of the MVP assets, as T2 states: the simulation reads the ports from the meshes' sockets (AST-043), so a recipe gives exact, repeatable port positions, and the owner designs each silhouette's recipe (recommended); (b) the owner models them in the owner's own tools and exports CMO with the socket bones, as for `LeaderFigure.cmo`, and T2 becomes a check of the sockets against the rows; (c) the kit for the collision assets and the port pad, the owner's own meshes for the rest.
4. **When the seen layer is built.** MVP-084's fog is M2's, and MVP-097 has the server compute the seen layer once, for the AI, the deputy and the replication.
