## 1 Purpose

This plan turns milestone M2 of the [MVP scope](mvp_scope.md), the settlement, into work an implementing session can start cold. It covers:
- what M2 owes, beyond the scope's own row (§2);
- how the first guesses of the scope's table 3 stand against M2's exit, before any code (§3);
- the order of the work (§4);
- the scripted steward and the growth measurement (§5, O80);
- the tasks of M2, each one or more changes on M2's pull request, as M1's were (§6, O71);
- the world the steward plays at M2 (§7);
- what is carried, open or interim at M2 (§8);
- the owner's answers to its four questions (§9, O81) and to the four the model raised (§9.1, O84);
- what the owner does during M2 (§10).

How work flows between the cloud session and the owner's PC is unchanged from the [M0 plan](m0_plan.md) §2: CI is the compiler, the owner runs what touches presentation, and nothing is claimed that was not done.

M2 ends when the exit criteria of the MVP scope's §3 hold:
- one settlement with no neighbours grows from 20 Workers to its plateau, with a growth step at least every `pil.settlement.growth_step_minutes` (20 minutes) of play in its first 5 hours, and plateaus after 4 to 6 hours of play;
- the steward finds it nice to watch for 10 minutes.

By O80, the scripted steward of §5 tunes the rules to the first criterion headless, and the owner's own play confirms it, measured by the server's statistics (MVP-064, pulled forward to M2 by the scope's amendment of table 2).

## 3 The first guesses against the exit

The scope's table 3 sets its first guesses against a plateau near 280 Workers paid for by the upkeep. The estimate made while this plan was written is now a model, `Tools/DesignModels/M2Economy.py`: one settlement under MVP-020 to MVP-038 on the site's 11 resource units (WLD-082, WLD-085, MVP-010), stepped one second at a time at the session pace under a greedy scripted steward (a storehouse at each of three places between the outer resource sites once the territory holds it, a dwelling within five Workers of the limit, no turret), each port worked by one gatherer that walks to the nearest drop-off and back at 20 wu/s over the valley path factor, and judged by O81's growth step (§9, question 1). It leaves out walking contention, the port search's draw, pathing round structures and the orbs' drift, so its sweep asks every set to meet the exit also with each gathering cycle a quarter longer. Its `check` reproduces every figure below, and its `sweep` the counts of the sets.

- **The first hours are bound by 12 ports.** The founding territory of 500 wu holds only the three inner home resource sites at 350 wu, 12 gathering ports that bring about 920 ru an hour.
- **The outer ring opens late.** The five outer sites at 800 wu enter the territory at 95 Workers (`mvp.territory.radius_per_worker` of 4 wu), which the model reaches after 4.1 hours of play.
- **The gaps are the waits at the population limit.** Below its limit the Wellspring turns every 20 ru into a Worker, so the stores fill only at the limit, and each dwelling is paid for by a wait at the slow income. With the first guesses the largest gap in the first 5 hours is 45 minutes, before the second dwelling. Keeping the dwelling's cost back from the Wellspring before the limit does not help (54 minutes, `variants`): the wait is set by the income, not by when the saving starts.
- **The plateau comes after the run.** The population reaches 280 at 7.6 hours and is still growing when the 8-hour run ends, against an exit of 4 to 6 hours.
- **The upkeep never binds.** At the end of the run the 44 ports bring about 4,000 ru an hour with storehouses near the outer sites, against an upkeep of about 2,000, so growth stops at `mvp.settlement.population_max`, not where the income and the upkeep meet. The scope's §4 counted about 60 ru per gatherer an hour (2,640 ru for 44), which leaves out the storehouses near the outer sites.
- **M3's first contact comes late.** The territory reaches the halfway line of a valley border pair (1,040 wu, WLD-064) at 5.6 hours, against the hour 2 to 3 that the scope's §4 sets the first guesses for and the [M3 plan](m3_plan.md) §3 builds on.

**The levers reach the exit only within the model's error.** The model's `sweep` tries the 1,600 sets of the first five of §9's levers over their ranges, `mvp.resource.ports_per_unit` held: 58 meet M2's exit as modelled, 13 of them also M3's window of hour 2 to 3, one meets the exit also with the margin, and none meets the exit with the margin and M3's window together.
- One set meets the exit with the margin: `mvp.territory.radius_per_worker` 12, `mvp.cost.dwelling_ru` 150 and `mvp.essence.orb_cost_ru` 15. Its largest gap is 15 minutes and its plateau comes at 4.1 hours, at 6.0 with the margin and at 3.0 with every cycle a fifth shorter, so its window is no wider than the model's error; and its territory reaches the halfway line at 0.9 hours, far too early for M3.
- The set preferred in §9's order that meets the exit and M3's window, `mvp.territory.radius_per_worker` 8 and `mvp.cost.dwelling_ru` 200, has a largest gap of exactly 20 minutes, its plateau at 5.1 hours and contact at 2.1 hours; with the margin its gap is 26 minutes and it reaches no plateau in the run.

Three consequences follow for this plan. The tuning is a task of its own (T10), run on the scripted steward with stated levers in a stated order before any presentation is built around a settlement of that shape (§9, question 2). M3 inherits the result: first contact between neighbours is timed by the same territory growth, so the M3 plan cannot be finished before T10 has run ([M3 plan](m3_plan.md) §3). And what yields when T10 cannot hold every target was the owner's to settle before T10 starts (§9.1, O84).

> **Amendment 2026-10-22 (M2 task T10; [ADR-138](../ADR/ADR-138-the-scripted-steward-and-the-growth-scene-at-m2.md)).** The growth scene measured the game's own settlement under the scripted steward on seeds 1 to 8. On the first guesses it reaches its plateau in half the model's time: 3.8 hours, against the model's 280 Workers at 7.6 hours. Gatherers take a port in turn: while one carries its load, the next mans the port. The model has one gatherer for each port, so its ports bring about 80 ru an hour each and stand idle while their gatherers walk.
> - On the first guesses, the largest gap was 17.3 to 17.8 minutes, the halfway line fell at 2.20 to 2.25 hours, and the plateau at `mvp.settlement.population_max` came at 3.81 to 3.85 hours, before the window. So the tuning started from the first guesses, not from the model's sweep.
> - Lever 1: `mvp.territory.radius_per_worker` at 3.5 put the plateau at 4.15 hours and the halfway line at 2.77, and at 3 the halfway line at 3.17. It delays contact about as much as the plateau, so it stays at 4.
> - Lever 2: `mvp.cost.dwelling_ru` at 350 or 400 made the largest gap 20 to 23 minutes. `mvp.cost.dwelling_escalation` below 1.15 shortens the last dwellings' waits, which are the largest gap, but brings the plateau earlier.
> - Lever 3: `mvp.essence.orb_cost_ru` delays every step alike.
> - The set taken, the escalation at 1.10 and the orb cost at 27, gives a largest gap of 14.9 to 15.4 minutes, a plateau at 4.27 to 4.32 hours and the halfway line at 2.65 to 2.67 hours. It keeps the widest margins on the step and the contact, which O84 holds before the plateau.
> - The model's figures above stand as what was estimated before the scene existed.

## 4 The order of M2

**The settlement comes first, headless and tuned, and the look follows.** As at M1, a measurement moves values that the later work is built around: here the shape of a settlement over a run, which sets the HUD's figures, how many structures the view draws and when contact arrives at M3. So the tasks fall in two phases, as M1's did:
- **Phase A, T2 to T10**, builds the settlement as a headless simulation. CI tests every task, and none needs a run checklist except T2, whose silhouettes the owner approves (approved as drafted, O87). It ends with T10, the scripted steward's growth scene and the tuning, and with the owner's run of the performance script on `Release|ARM64`.
- **Phase B, T11 to T19**, makes the settlement visible and playable. Every task states a run checklist (M0 plan §2) and is not merged before the owner's answer.

T2 and T3 come first because the simulation reads the structures' ports and footprints from the cooked simulation data records (AST-043, AST-046), never from a render mesh (ADR-021); until they land, T4 and T5 test on records written by the tests. T14 and T15, the UI framework and the settings store, depend on nothing in phase A and may run beside it, as M1's content build did (O73), each pushed with its run checklist; the HUD, the verbs and the front end (T16 to T18) follow T10, so that the tuning settles the figures they show.

**Phase A starts before M1's exit (O86).** Phase A's tasks land on M2's own pull request, stacked on M1's T17c, while M1's last exit evidence is still to come; phase B waits for M1's exit, and this plan stays a Draft until then. The recipes of T2 are drafted by the implementing session from the [MVP assets](mvp_assets.md) and approved or redesigned by the owner before T2 merges. The owner approved them as drafted on 2026-10-22, after the phase's pull request merged (O87, `playtests/M2.md`).

**Phase B starts before M1's exit too (O88).** Phase B's tasks land on one pull request of their own, from T11 in the order of table 2, each pushed with its run checklist and not merged before the owner's answer. M1's frame interval, above O75's 17.7 ms at the 99th percentile on the owner's first rendered run while the frame's own work was 6.10 ms, is taken up at T19, whose rendered scene is judged against the same target. The M2 files the owner has not made yet are the content build's generated stand-ins ([GameAssets.md](../assets/GameAssets.md) §2.6) until the owner's files replace them.

## 5 The scripted steward and the growth measurement

O80 has a scripted steward tune M2 and the owner's play confirm it.

**The scripted steward is the economy half of an AI settlement.** It is written in GameLogic as a controller that reads only its own settlement and the public layer, sends its commands as an AI sender through the one command path of ADR-087, executed at the next tick (MVP-094, DEC-122), and holds its state in the snapshot, so that M3's AI settlements (MVP-056) extend it rather than start again. Its policy is fixed and stated in its task: the default priorities of MVP-036, a dwelling queued when the population comes within a stated margin of its limit, a storehouse placed between outer resource sites once the territory holds them, and a placement layout that keeps every port and path clear. It never reads anything the steward's client could not see.

**The growth scene** runs in `Tools/SpeciesPerformance` on the game's libraries, as M1's headless scenes do (ADR-110): one settlement at the steward's site with no neighbours, from a fixed seed and the scripted steward, for `mvp.run.length_hours` of play, stepped as fast as the machine allows. It reports, from the server's statistics (MVP-064):
- every growth step with its tick, and the largest gap between two of them in the first 5 hours;
- the time at which the plateau is reached, by the definition the task states (no growth step and a Worker count within a stated share of its maximum for an hour of play);
- the Worker count, the stores, the population limit and the strength (MVP-038) every `mvp.stats.sample_minutes`;
- the share of Workers idle by each reason of MVP-037.

It runs on a corpus of seeds, because every site has the same distances (WLD-042, WLD-082) but not the same slopes or paths. **In CI** a shortened scene of the first hour of play runs as a regression: its per-tick hashes are stable and its growth steps fall inside the bounds the task records. **On the reference test machine** the performance script runs the whole scene and writes its figures into `playtests/M2.md`.

**The owner's confirmation** is the owner's own play from 20 Workers to the plateau, across sessions that save and continue (MVP-075), with the same statistics recorded by the server and compared with the scene's.

## 9 The owner's answers

The owner took this plan's recommendation on each of its four questions (O81, 2026-10-21); the decision records the options that were offered.

1. **A growth step** is a structure completed, the population reaching each new multiple of 10 Workers, or the territory taking in a resource site it did not hold: each is a moment the steward sees. PIL-055's gain of territory, taken literally, would be met by every new Worker (MVP-026). The population step is a value held in data, which T8 adds with its key to the scope's table 3 and the tuning record; it is fixed before T10 runs and is not one of T10's levers, so that the tuning cannot meet the criterion by redefining a step.
2. **T10 moves only the `mvp.*` keys**, in this order: `mvp.territory.radius_per_worker` (when the outer ring opens), `mvp.cost.dwelling_ru` and `mvp.cost.dwelling_escalation` (the wait at each limit), `mvp.essence.orb_cost_ru` (the rate of growth), the upkeep keys (so that the plateau is where income and upkeep meet, below `mvp.settlement.population_max`), and last `mvp.resource.ports_per_unit`, which changes the resource-site meshes and so T2's recipes. A value of the Final world design (WLD-060, WLD-082) that needs to move goes to the owner.
3. **The mesh kit makes the structure meshes**, as T2 states: the simulation reads the ports from the meshes' sockets (AST-043), so a recipe gives exact, repeatable port positions. The owner designs each silhouette's recipe and approves the result.
4. **The seen layer is built at M2, on the server** (MVP-016, MVP-097): T6 computes it once per update, T11 replicates it and T13 draws the fog from it. With one settlement it hides nothing that M2 sends; M3 adds its readers, the AI settlements' navigation graph and priority field (MVP-058) and the replication narrowed to it (ADR-111), with their tests.

### 9.1 The questions the model raised

The model of §3 raised four more questions. The owner took this plan's recommendation on each (O84, 2026-10-21), ahead of M1's exit; the decision records the options that were offered.

5. **What gives way.** `mvp.territory.radius_per_worker` times both the outer ring, which M2's exit needs early, and M3's first contact, which the scope wants in hour 2 to 3, and the model finds sets that meet M2's exit and M3's window together only within its own error (§3). T10 may therefore find no set that holds all three of a step every 20 minutes, a plateau in hours 4 to 6 and contact in hour 2 to 3, and it needs to know which one yields first. **The plateau window yields first**: the step of PIL-055 is what a steward feels in every session, and Playtest 1's battles need contact in hour 2 to 3, while the plateau's hour is felt once a run. The step and the contact hold, and the plateau may fall outside hours 4 to 6, though within the run (the scope's table 2, amended).
6. **The plateau at the cap.** With every set the model finds, growth stops at `mvp.settlement.population_max` because the income at full territory is nearly twice the upkeep. Raising the per-Worker upkeep does not bring the two together inside the run, because it eats the early income first: for the set of §3 that meets both windows, the largest gap grows to 32 minutes at 11 ru an hour and to 70 at 14, and the income still exceeds the upkeep when the run ends. So the diminishing return of PIL-053 can come from the upkeep only if the upkeep rises faster than the population, which is a rule of B2's that MVP-035 does not have. **A plateau at the cap is accepted for the MVP**: M2's exit asks for a plateau, not for its cause, and the escalating dwelling cost already slows the last hours. An upkeep that brings the plateau below the cap is B2's (the scope's §4, amended).
7. **Where a gatherer looks for a port.** MVP-031 bounds the gathering port search by `ref.unit.worker.port_search_range`, the reference's 100 wu (R-UNIT-090), but the nearest resource sites lie 350 wu from the Wellspring, so a gatherer there finds no port unless it has wandered within 100 wu of one. The scope's own figure of 60 ru per gatherer an hour and the model both assume a gatherer that walks to a free port anywhere in its territory. **The gathering search covers the territory**, nearest free port first, and the 100 wu stays the range of the idle Worker's port search (MVP-031, amended).
8. **How T10 judges a miss.** The model is good to about a third, and a gathering cycle a quarter longer moves the plateau by about two hours (§3). **T10 tunes to its own measurement** on the game's libraries, over its corpus of seeds, and brings question 5's answer into play only where that measurement misses; the windows are not widened on the model's figures.
